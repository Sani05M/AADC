About the Program

The Faculty Development Program on Literature, Language and Media is meticulously designed to bridge the intersections between traditional humanities and the evolving digital landscape. This program focuses intensely on the synthesis of Literature, Language, and Media in the contemporary academic ecosystem.

Participants will engage with advanced concepts in Digital Humanities, Digital Culture, and Archives. The curriculum is tailored to equip modern educators and researchers with robust methodologies for Scholarly Publishing, nuanced Media Studies, advanced Research Methodology, and sustainable Digital Preservation techniques.

Reports

Daily Session Details

Detailed academic reports from each day of the Faculty Development Program.

Day 1

Archiving and Digital Humanities

Dr. Shantanu Majhee
+

Dr. Shantanu Majhee, Techno India University

The inaugural session focused profoundly on the intersections of archival research and Digital Humanities. Dr. Majhee elaborated on how traditional literary studies are evolving through computational methodologies. The session explored the digitization of classical texts, metadata standardization, and the philosophical implications of digital curation.

"The archive is not merely a repository, but an active agent in shaping literary historiography."

Participants engaged in hands-on theoretical frameworks analyzing how digital tools reconstruct our understanding of historical narratives.

Day 2

Digital Culture & Archives

Dr. Maya Dodd
+

Dr. Maya Dodd, FLAME University

Dr. Maya Dodd presented an incisive analysis of Digital Culture and its reciprocal relationship with archival practices. The session unpacked the democratization of knowledge through open-access archives and the ethical complexities of digitizing indigenous cultural artifacts.

Significant time was dedicated to understanding the pedagogical shifts required to teach digital culture in modern Indian classrooms, aligning with the multidisciplinary ethos of NEP 2020.

Day 3

Intersection of Language & Media

Dr. Rimi Nandy
+

Dr. Rimi Nandy, Jadavpur University

This session provided a robust framework for examining how contemporary media platforms influence linguistic evolution. Dr. Nandy discussed socio-linguistic transformations triggered by digital communication, analyzing memes, digital vernaculars, and platform-specific rhetorics.

Day 4

Scholarly Publishing in Humanities

Prof. Jayati Gupta
+

Prof. Jayati Gupta, West Bengal State University

An essential session for researchers aiming to navigate the complex landscape of academic publishing. Prof. Gupta covered journal selection strategies, navigating peer-review, understanding impact factors versus qualitative reach, and the rising paradigm of open-access humanities journals.

Day 5

Postcolonial Archives

Dr. Sanjukta Ghosh
+

Dr. Sanjukta Ghosh, SOAS University of London

Dr. Ghosh offered a brilliant deconstruction of imperial archival practices and the necessity for postcolonial counter-archives. The lecture emphasized recovering marginalized voices and re-reading colonial documents against the grain using modern digital archival tools.

Day 6

Digital Preservation Methods

Dr. Debapriya Basu
+

Dr. Debapriya Basu, IIT Guwahati

Focusing on the technical and methodological aspects of archiving, Dr. Basu introduced the participants to metadata standards (Dublin Core), optical character recognition (OCR) challenges with regional languages, and the architecture of sustainable digital repositories.

Day 7

Ecolinguistics & Media

Dr. Rahi Soren
+

Dr. Rahi Soren, Jadavpur University

A fascinating exploration into how ecological concerns intersect with language and media representations. Dr. Soren highlighted the discourse of climate change in literature and the role of digital media in shaping ecological consciousness and environmental narratives.

Day 8

Multilingual Digital Humanities

Prof. Nirmala Menon
+

Prof. Nirmala Menon, IIT Indore

Addressing the Anglocentric bias in early Digital Humanities, Prof. Menon advocated for robust multilingual digital infrastructures. The session detailed projects involving Indian language text mining, translation databases, and preserving linguistic diversity in the digital age.

Day 9

Media Narratives & Society

Partho Chakraborty
+

Partho Chakraborty, Media Professional

Bringing an industry perspective, Mr. Chakraborty analyzed the construction of media narratives and their socio-political impact. The discussion spanned from traditional journalism to algorithmic curation in social media, challenging attendees to critically evaluate information ecosystems.

Day 10

Gender & Digital Archives

Dr. Priyanka Tripathi
+

Dr. Priyanka Tripathi, IIT Patna

Dr. Tripathi examined the intersection of gender studies and digital archiving. The session highlighted how digital platforms can subvert patriarchal historical narratives by providing accessible spaces for feminist archives, women's histories, and intersectional storytelling.

Day 11

Decolonial Archiving Practices

Dr. Sayan Dey
+

Dr. Sayan Dey, Alliance University

An intense theoretical session focusing on decoloniality within the context of knowledge production. Dr. Dey argued for epistemic disobedience in archival research, pushing researchers to look beyond Eurocentric epistemologies when cataloging and studying indigenous literatures.

Day 12

Research Methodology in Media

Dr. Rajdeep Roy
+

Dr. Rajdeep Roy, Adamas University

The concluding academic session provided a pragmatic overview of qualitative and quantitative research methodologies specifically tailored for media studies and literature. Topics included discourse analysis, ethnographic media research, and formulating impactful research proposals.
